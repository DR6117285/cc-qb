# NBME One-Best-Answer Item Writing Rules

Source: Derived from NBME item writing standards and the principles document supplied for LMQBank.

---

## Structural rules

### Stem / vignette
1. Write a clinical or applied scenario: include patient age, relevant background, presenting situation or findings
2. Every detail in the vignette must be clinically necessary — there is no irrelevant information
3. The stem must allow the candidate to formulate the answer BEFORE seeing the options ("cover-the-options test")
4. For hard questions: introduce a complicating factor (comorbidity, contradictory finding, atypical presentation) that requires the candidate to prioritise one principle over another

### Lead-in question
5. One clear, closed question
6. Must be singular and specific — not compound
7. No negative phrasing: never use EXCEPT, NOT, or LEAST
8. Typical examples:
   - "Which of the following is the most appropriate next step?"
   - "Which of the following best explains...?"
   - "Which intervention has the strongest evidence for...?"
   - "Which of the following is most consistent with...?"

### Answer options
9.  Exactly 4 options (A–D), labelled as "A) ...", "B) ...", "C) ...", "D) ..."
10. All options must be **homogeneous** — same category (e.g. all dietary interventions, all diagnoses, all mechanisms, all drug classes)
11. One option is the clearly best answer; the remaining three are plausible distractors
12. Distractors must represent:
    - Common misconceptions in the field
    - Correct answers to a slightly different clinical question
    - Evidence-based interventions that are correct but less effective or appropriate in this specific context
    - Never absurd, never clearly wrong at a glance
13. All options must be similar in length and grammatical structure
14. No "all of the above" or "none of the above"
15. No absolute terms in any option ("always", "never", "only")
16. The correct answer must NOT be obviously longer, more detailed, or more qualified than the distractors

## Difficulty calibration

### Hard (board-level) — default for LMQBank
- Tests application or clinical decision-making, NOT recall
- The question requires the candidate to USE knowledge in a novel scenario
- A competent but non-expert candidate should be genuinely uncertain between at least 2–3 options
- Numeric data (percentage reductions, study thresholds, RR values) make effective hard-question material
- Study-specific evidence (named trials with specific outcomes) challenges candidates who know the general principle but not the evidence base

### Medium
- Tests interpretation — the candidate must apply a principle to a scenario
- At least 1–2 distractors are genuinely tempting

### Easy (avoid unless deliberate)
- Pure recall — the candidate only needs to remember a definition or fact

## Cover-the-options test (mandatory check)

Read the vignette and lead-in. Without looking at the options, ask: "Can a knowledgeable candidate identify the correct answer?"

If the answer is "no" or "only with options visible", the stem needs revision.

Common failures:
- Vignette is too vague to point toward a specific answer
- Lead-in asks for something too open-ended ("which of the following is true about X?")
- The complicating factor changes the answer so dramatically it cannot be anticipated

## Rationale format

After each question, provide:

1. **Correct answer explanation** (2–3 sentences): explain WHY this answer is best, citing the specific evidence, guideline, or mechanism
2. **Each distractor explanation**: why it is wrong but plausible

Format:
```
"[Option X] is correct: [explanation with evidence/mechanism]. [Option A] is incorrect: [reason — why wrong and why plausible]. [Option B] is incorrect: [reason]. [Option C] is incorrect: [reason]."
```

## Common item-writing errors to avoid

| Error | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Negative phrasing | "Which of the following is NOT a risk factor..." | Rephrase as positive |
| Non-homogeneous options | Mix of drugs, dietary patterns, and procedures | Keep all options in same category |
| Absurd distractors | Options that no competent clinician would choose | Replace with plausible misconceptions |
| Correct answer stands out | Only option with qualifying language or much longer | Make all options parallel length |
| Double-barrelled stem | "Which is the most effective AND cheapest?" | One criterion only |
| Absolute language | "Exercise always reduces HbA1c" | Remove absolute terms |
| Irrelevant vignette detail | Patient's occupation when not clinically relevant | Remove unless it affects the answer |
| Stem doesn't focus | "A patient presents. What do you do?" | Write a specific closed lead-in |
